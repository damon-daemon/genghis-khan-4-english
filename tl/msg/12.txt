@12000
Head of the Minamoto clan. Raised an army in Izu and, with Yoshitsune's help, destroyed the Taira. As Seii Taishogun he founded the Kamakura shogunate.

@12001
Younger brother of Minamoto no Yoritomo. Distinguished himself against the Taira, but Yoritomo, fearing his rise, hunted him down and he killed himself in Oshu.

@12002
Retainer of Minamoto no Yoshitsune. A fine warrior who always followed him. Fell in battle at Hiraizumi, defending Yoshitsune against Yoritomo's army.

@12003
Younger brother of Minamoto no Yoritomo. Fought Kiso Yoshinaka and the Taira with Yoshitsune. Yoritomo suspected him of treason, exiled him to Izu and had him killed.

@12004
Father of Hojo Masako, wife of Minamoto no Yoritomo. Backed Yoritomo's war on the Taira. After Yoritomo died he ousted the heir, became regent and seized power.

@12005
Son of Hojo Tokimasa. Won renown against the Oshu Fujiwara. After Yoritomo's death he confined Yoriie in Izu and succeeded his father as the 2nd regent.

@12006
Son of Hojo Yoshitoki. Crushed the Retired Emperor Go-Toba's rebels and became the 3rd regent. A gifted statesman, he issued the Goseibai Shikimoku law code.

@12007
A former court official. Invited by Minamoto no Yoritomo, he became an official of the Kamakura shogunate, serving mainly as its negotiator with the imperial court.

@12008
Said to be the ideal Kamakura warrior. Upright and pure, a fine fighter, and gifted in song and dance. He was reportedly killed by a plot of the Hojo clan.

@12009
6th emperor of the Jin. A man of culture. He worked to improve the institutions, but Yellow River floods and Mongol invasions weakened the state.

@12010
7th emperor of the Jin. Took the throne after Zhangzong's death. His reign was unstable with the Mongols breaking in, and after five years he was murdered by his subject.

@12011
A former Jin official. Sent to the Mongols as an envoy, he met Genghis Khan and became his vassal. Fluent in the languages of many lands, he served mainly in diplomacy.

@12012
A great minister of the Mongol Empire. Once a Jin official, he became Genghis Khan's vassal. Under Ogedei he set up local administration and taxation.

@12013
Chancellor of the Jin. Led the Jin army against the Mongols. When the capital Yanjing was besieged he was left isolated and finally killed himself with poison.

@12014
A renowned Taoist of the Quanzhen school. Denying the elixir of long life, he said "There is a way to guard health, but none to live forever," and counseled Genghis Khan.

@12015
A Jin general. Relieved of command for failing to put down the revolt of the Khitan Yelu Liuge.

@12016
A Jin general. Rebelled in Liaodong and, with Mongol aid, beat back the Jin punitive army. Genghis Khan granted him the title King of Liao.

@12017
19th king of Goryeo. Rallied and led the military cliques in a coup and took the throne, but was later deposed by Choe Chung-heon, one of those cliques' leaders.

@12018
A Goryeo civil official gifted in diplomacy. When the Mongols invaded Goryeo, he sent a petition to Ogedei Khan and succeeded in making the army withdraw.

@12019
A Goryeo general of the middle period. Rose by winning the struggle among the military cliques, deposed King Myeongjong, set up puppet kings, and ruled as he pleased.

@12020
A Goryeo general. Repelled the Mongol invasion, but other ministers envied his merit and had him murdered.

@12021
2nd emperor of the Southern Song. Made peace with the Jin, reformed the civil service and currency, and developed Jiangnan. He led the Southern Song to its peak.

@12022
Son of Xiaozong. Became the 3rd Southern Song emperor, Guangzong. Lacking talent, he let Empress Li run wild, and after only five years he was forced into retirement.

@12023
A Southern Song statesman. Led the peace faction on the Jin question, drove out the war faction, and won a treaty. As chancellor he held dictatorial power for 26 years.

@12024
A Southern Song scholar of Zhu Xi's school. Recommended by Emperor Lizong, he worked tirelessly in government and left many writings. Called "Little Zhu Xi."

@12025
A Southern Song Confucian. He synthesized Song Confucian philosophy and promoted "Zhu Xi learning," which stresses the Four Books alongside the Five Classics.

@12026
A late Southern Song official. Made chancellor for holding off the Mongol invasion. After rising high he grew tyrannical, and was killed when he lost to the Mongols.

@12027
A Southern Song statesman and scholar who served Xiaozong. Especially learned in geography. His works include "Yiyuan" and "Beibian."

@12028
A Southern Song Confucian. Clashed with Zhu Xi over the interpretation of Confucianism. He taught that "the mind is principle," which influenced the Ming school of Wang Yangming.

@12029
A monk of the Sakya school of Tibetan Buddhism (Lamaism). Revered and protected by Genghis Khan, he laid the groundwork for the Sakya school's later prosperity.

@12030
A monk of the Phagmodrupa school of Tibetan Buddhism (Lamaism). Worked hard to bring about peace between the Mongols and Tibet.

@12031
A monk of the Sakya school of Tibetan Buddhism (Lamaism). Foreseeing the Mongol invasion of Tibet, he warned his nephew Sakya Pandita.

@12032
First khan of the Mongol Empire. After uniting the Mongols he campaigned against Western Xia, Jin and Khwarezm and built a vast empire.

@12033
Eldest son of Genghis Khan. Suspected of having a different father, he was named Jochi ("guest"). His place in the family was complicated, but he was a brave commander.

@12034
Second son of Genghis Khan. Hot-tempered, he often clashed with his brother Jochi. Joined his father's western campaign and was granted lands in Central Asia.

@12035
2nd khan of the Mongol Empire, third son of Genghis Khan. He relied on Yelu Chucai to organize the government and sent armies against the Jin and Europe.

@12036
Son of Jochi. Became commander of the European expedition. He crushed the Polish-German forces at Legnica and founded the Khanate of the Golden Horde (Kipchak).

@12037
One of the "Four Steeds." When Genghis Khan's horses were stolen in his boyhood, he helped him and became his vassal. The oldest of his veteran retainers.

@12038
Younger brother of Genghis Khan. As his name Khasar ("fierce") suggests, he was a mighty warrior. Fought hard against the Naiman, then died in camp in the Jin campaign.

@12039
The brother Genghis Khan loved most. Distinguished himself against the Naiman and in the Jin campaign. After Ogedei's death he went for the khanate but failed.

@12040
One of the "Four Hounds." When Genghis Khan was wounded in battle, he ran through the enemy camp to steal water and nursed him all night.

@12041
One of the "Four Hounds." Against the Naiman he broke through the mountains with iron-wheeled carts alongside his brother Jelme. Joined the western campaign.

@12042
One of the "Four Steeds." When the Tayichiud hunted the young Genghis Khan, he hid him and earned his trust. His descendants were one of the four great Yuan noble clans.

@12043
One of the "Four Hounds." When Genghis Khan broke with Jamukha, he came over to his side. After the Mongols were united, he was given command of military administration.

@12044
One of the "Four Steeds." Found on a battlefield and raised by Genghis Khan's mother. Rescued the dying Ogedei from the field. Fell in battle against the Tumat.

@12045
Once Genghis Khan's sworn friend and his greatest rival. A wise and brave commander. Even when captured he refused to submit, and was finally executed.

@12046
Jamukha's own brother. Shot dead by Genghis Khan's men while trying to steal horses. This incident began the Battle of the Thirteen Wings between Genghis Khan and Jamukha.

@12047
One of the "Four Hounds." A master archer. Even as a captive he kept his dignity, so Genghis Khan prized him and made him a vassal, one of the army's fiercest generals.

@12048
Chief of the Naiman. Introduced oasis culture and built a major power in western Mongolia. Allied with Jamukha to face Genghis Khan, but was defeated and killed in battle.

@12049
Son of the Naiman chief Tayang Khan. When his father fell to Genghis Khan, he fled to the Western Liao and seized its throne. Defeated by Jebe, he was executed.
